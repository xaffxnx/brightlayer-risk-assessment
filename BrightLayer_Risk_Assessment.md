# BrightLayer Stores — Cybersecurity Risk Memo

## 1. FIVE IMPORTANT ASSETS

1. **Employee Email Accounts** — Used for business communication, customer and vendor contact, and password-reset processes. Compromised email accounts could expose sensitive information and allow attackers to impersonate employees.

2. **Shared Computers** — Employees use shared computers to access company systems and accounts. These devices may contain saved credentials, business documents, or active browser sessions.

3. **Company Website** — The public website represents BrightLayer Stores online and may provide information or services to customers. Website compromise could damage the company's reputation and availability.

4. **Cloud Storage with Invoices** — Contains business invoices and potentially sensitive financial or customer information. Unauthorized access, loss, or disclosure could cause financial and privacy impacts.

5. **User Passwords and Public Wi-Fi Router** — Passwords protect company accounts, while the Wi-Fi router provides network access. Weak passwords or an insecure router could provide attackers with unauthorized access to business resources.

## 2. THREE POTENTIAL THREATS

1. **Phishing/Cybercriminal** — An attacker may send fake emails to employees to steal passwords or gain access to company accounts.

2. **Malware Attacker** — A malicious file or compromised website could infect a shared computer and steal credentials or business information.

3. **Unauthorized Network Attacker** — Someone near the business could attempt to exploit the public Wi-Fi router to access the internal network or intercept traffic.

## 3. THREE VULNERABILITIES

1. **Weak or reused user passwords** — Employees may use simple or reused passwords without multi-factor authentication (MFA).

2. **Insufficient security on shared computers** — Shared systems may have saved browser passwords, excessive user privileges, outdated software, or inadequate endpoint protection.

3. **Insecure Wi-Fi/router configuration** — The router may use weak security settings, outdated firmware, default administrator credentials, or allow unnecessary network access.

## 4. ASSOCIATED RISKS

1. **Phishing + weak/reused passwords → Account compromise** — An attacker who obtains an employee password could access email or cloud storage, steal invoices, reset other accounts, impersonate employees, and cause financial or reputational loss.

2. **Malware + insufficient shared-computer security → Data theft or business disruption** — Malware could steal saved credentials or confidential invoices, damage files, or make computers unavailable, affecting daily operations.

3. **Unauthorized network attacker + insecure Wi-Fi/router → Unauthorized network access** — An attacker could gain access to connected devices or intercept poorly protected traffic, potentially exposing company data and disrupting network services.

## 5. SECURITY RECOMMENDATIONS

1. **Enable MFA** for employee email and cloud-storage accounts to reduce the impact of stolen passwords. *(Risk 1)*

2. **Implement strong, unique passwords** and use a password manager instead of reusing passwords. *(Risk 1)*

3. **Apply least privilege on shared computers**, remove unnecessary administrator access, and prevent browsers from saving sensitive passwords. *(Risk 2)*

4. **Keep operating systems, browsers, applications, and endpoint security tools updated** to reduce malware exploitation. *(Risk 2)*

5. **Secure the Wi-Fi router** by changing default administrator credentials, using WPA2/WPA3, disabling unnecessary services, and keeping firmware updated. *(Risk 3)*

6. **Separate guest/public Wi-Fi from business devices** using a guest network or network segmentation. *(Risk 3)*

7. **Provide phishing-awareness training** and teach employees to verify suspicious links, attachments, and login requests. *(Risk 1)*
